# Off-site checklist for AI visibility (reviews, roundups, Reddit, YouTube, digital PR)

**AI engines often answer from pages you don't own, so earn a genuine place on the sites they list as sources for your prompts.** First, find those sites by tracking your prompts. Then work on them in order of how often they appear: review platforms, "best X" roundups, Reddit threads, YouTube videos, and press. Do it honestly. Paid or fake mentions break platform rules and consumer law.

IDs match the [master checklist](https://github.com/Cited-io/ai-seo-geo-checklist/blob/main/CHECKLIST.md).

## What our dataset shows

Cited publishes an open [AI search citations dataset](https://github.com/Cited-io/ai-search-citations-dataset). Its 2026-10 release covers 138 answers: 23 prompts about AI search, GEO and AI visibility tools, asked on 6 engines from the US on 2026-10-14. Your category will have its own source list, so treat these as an example of the pattern, not as your numbers. Three facts from it:

- **The source pool is wide.** Across the 138 answers, engines listed 566 distinct domains as sources.
- **Engines differ a lot.** Average domains listed as sources per answer:

  | Engine | Domains per answer |
  |---|---|
  | ChatGPT | 17.2 |
  | Claude | 6.0 |
  | Microsoft Copilot | 4.7 |
  | Gemini | 4.2 |
  | Google AI Overviews | 3.8 |
  | Google AI Mode | 2.6 |

- **Community sites show up.** reddit.com was listed as a source in 21 answers and youtube.com in 13. Google AI Overviews leaned on them most: it listed youtube.com in 43.5% of its answers and reddit.com in 39.1%.

What it means in practice: on ChatGPT, being one of many listed sources is achievable. On Google AI Mode, two or three sources carry the whole answer, so the top results decide.

## Items

### O1. You know which sites engines list as sources for your prompts
- **Do:** for each tracked prompt and engine, record the domains and URLs the answer lists (see M5 in [measurement](https://github.com/Cited-io/ai-seo-geo-checklist/blob/main/checklists/measurement.md)). Count how often each domain appears across all answers.
- **Result:** a ranked target list. A roundup that appears in, say, 30% of answers and doesn't name you is worth more than another post on your own blog.

### O2. You plan per engine
- **Do:** split the target list by engine. A domain that matters on ChatGPT may never appear on Google AI Mode.

### O3. You collect genuine reviews on the sites your buyers read
- **Where:** the review sites that show up in your source list. Examples: G2 or Capterra for software, Google and Yelp for local services, Trustpilot for consumer brands, app stores for apps.
- **Rules:**
  - The US FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews and incentives "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment". [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)
  - Google Maps bans incentives for reviews and selectively soliciting positive ones. It also bans reviews from employees or others with a conflict of interest. [Google contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)
- **Do:** ask every customer at a natural moment, with a direct link. Reply to reviews, good and bad.

### O4. You are on the "best X" roundups engines cite *(practice)*
- **Do:**
  - find the lists in your source data;
  - check each list's update cadence and contact;
  - send the author what they need to evaluate you: pricing, a trial or demo account, differentiators, customer proof.
- **Be honest:** if a placement is paid, it should be disclosed on the list. Prefer lists that test products.

### O5. You take part in Reddit honestly
- **Why:** reddit.com was listed as a source in 21 of 138 answers in our dataset (questions about AI search and GEO), and in 39.1% of Google AI Overviews answers.
- **Rules:** Reddit's Rule 2 asks users to "participate authentically in communities where you have a personal interest, and do not spam or engage in disruptive behaviors (including content manipulation)". Each subreddit adds its own rules on self-promotion. [Reddit Rules](https://redditinc.com/policies/reddit-rules)
- **Do:**
  - answer questions in your field from a named account that discloses your affiliation;
  - link only when the link is the best answer;
  - never buy upvotes or post as fake customers.

### O6. You publish YouTube videos that answer buyer questions
- **Why:** youtube.com was listed as a source in 13 of 138 answers in our dataset (questions about AI search and GEO), and in 43.5% of Google AI Overviews answers.
- **Do:**
  - **Titles:** phrase them as the question, such as "Which payroll software suits a 20-person team?".
  - **Chapters:** add timestamps in the description. The first must be `00:00`, with at least three chapters of 10 seconds or more. [YouTube: chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)
  - **Captions:** upload or correct them, so the spoken words are available as text. [YouTube: captions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796)
  - **Description:** state the answer in the first lines.

### O7. Digital PR is built on something worth citing
- **Why:** Google says "seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem". [Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- **Do:** publish original data, a survey, a benchmark or a useful tool, then pitch it to journalists and newsletters in your field. Expert commentary on news in your field also earns real mentions.

### O8. No link schemes
- **Why:** Google defines link spam as "creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings". [Google: spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- **Avoid:** bought links, link exchanges, and sitewide footer links on partner sites.
